# Hookfly bot

A bot you ping in chat to hookfly, 2-tile aled, pseudofly, and hammerhit with you. Its brain (`CHookBotBrain`, `src/game/hookbot.cpp`) only sees plain tee state, and two front ends feed it:
- **Client-side (default): it drives your own dummy** (`CGameClient::HookBotInput`, `cl_hookbot 1`). When the client sends the dummy's input for tick P, the predicted world holds both of your tees at the end of P-1. That is exactly what the server will see, because both inputs are local. So your prediction stays exact, and it works on any server.
- **Server-side:** a debug dummy computed in the server (`src/game/server/hookbot.cpp`, `sv_hookbot 1`). Your client doesn't know its inputs and mispredicts it whenever it hooks or jumps. The `SimBot.*` tests use it.

## Running
```sh
cd build-client && ninja -j2 game-client
cd build-sim && ninja -j2 DDNet-Server testrunner
scripts/run_hookbot_server.sh          # local server on the test map (port 8303), no server-side bot
```
Connect to `localhost:8303`, connect your dummy, and type `Claude hookfly` in chat. The bot takes over the dummy (you keep controlling your main tee) and renames it to `cl_hookbot_name` with skin `cl_hookbot_skin` for the session; your saved dummy settings stay as they are. It needs `cl_predict_dummy 1` (the default). Its replies are local chat echoes, only you see them. Turn on the entities overlay to see the freeze (`cl_overlay_entities 100`); the test map has no design layer.

Test map (`SimBot.WriteMap`): you spawn in an open shaft (plain hookfly). Walk right through the gaps at the floor to reach three shafts with a 2-tile freeze ceiling 10, 20 and 30 tiles above the floor, then a 200-tile-wide hall for flying sideways. `run_hookbot_server.sh` only writes the map if `build-sim/maps/hookbot.map` is missing; delete it to get a new layout.

## Skin
The bot wears `claude` (`cl_hookbot_skin` / `sv_hookbot_skin`): a terracotta tee with an ivory spark, `data/skins/claude.png`, generated from `default.png`'s shapes by `scripts/make_claude_skin.py`. Skins are client-side: a client without the file shows the default tee, so copy it into the client's `skins/` folder (on macOS `~/Library/Application Support/DDNet/skins/`).

## Video of a sim run
1. Record the run: `SIM_DEMO_DIR=<dir> HH_DEMO=<name>` on `SimMapBots.Hammerhit` writes `<dir>/<name>.demo`. Tee 0 is client id 127 and tee 1 is 126. Both are named Claude and wear `claude`.
2. Build the client with `-DVIDEORECORDER=ON`.
3. Put the map in the client's `maps/` under the name the demo asks for (the sim's runs say `tasmap`) and `claude.png` in `skins/`.
4. Render, headless under Xvfb (Mesa llvmpipe):
   ```sh
   DDNET_DEMO_RENDER_WAIT_MS=8000 DDNET_READ_BACK_BUFFER=1 DISPLAY=:99 ./DDNet "gfx_backend OpenGL; gfx_gl_major 3; gfx_gl_minor 3; gfx_screen_width 1280; gfx_screen_height 720; cl_default_zoom 12; snd_enable 0; cl_video_sound_enable 0; player_skin claude; demo_render demos/<name>.demo <name> 4 1 127"
   ```
   The video lands in `videos/<name>.mp4`. Speed index 4 is normal speed, 1 quits when done, and 127 follows tee 0.

Why each setting:
- **`DDNET_READ_BACK_BUFFER=1`:** the recorder reads frames from the front buffer, which is black under Xvfb, so this makes it read the back buffer.
- **The GL 3.3 settings:** without them Mesa gives a GL 1.1 context.
- **`player_skin claude` and `DDNET_DEMO_RENDER_WAIT_MS`:** they load the skin before playback starts. Without them the default tee showed for the first two minutes of the video, because skins load in the background and this one was first asked for once playback ran.

## Chat commands
Any message containing the bot's name (`cl_hookbot_name` / `sv_hookbot_name`, default `Claude`, case-insensitive). The client-side bot listens to your own tees and to the players in `cl_hookbot_friends` (comma-separated, default `always`). It plays with whoever gave the last command. When that's a friend and `cl_hookbot_chat` is 1 (default 0), it answers in real chat from the dummy so they can see it; otherwise its replies stay local echoes. Its view of a friend is only the usual prediction of another player (their inputs aren't known), so aled timing with a friend is less exact than with you. While your own tee is paused, spectating or dead, the client's prediction stands still (`UpdatePrediction` needs your character) and your connection may not even see the dummy, so the bot builds its view from the dummy connection's own snapshots instead (`CGameClient::HookBotWorld`); you can `/pause` or `/spec` and let a friend play with it. A friend's fire button isn't sent to other clients, so its swings (for the gestures) are rebuilt from its tee's attack tick. A friend in another DDRace team is invisible to it. `cl_hookbot_log 1` (the default) writes what the bot saw and did every tick to `hookbot_log.txt` in the save directory, a new file each client start:
- `Claude hookfly`: stand next to it. It jumps first and hooks you up, then you alternate.
- `Claude hookfly 2 tile aled` (anything with `aled`): the same hookfly, but under a freeze ceiling it flies only as high as is safe, then launches you through the freeze (letting go, holding on, or hammering you up). It dives in behind you and hammers you free on its first tick inside the freeze. It ends up frozen itself; hook it up and hammer it back if you can.
- `Claude pseudofly`: it hammers and you drive. Jump, hook it and steer; it stays under you and hammers you up whenever its reload allows.
- `Claude pseudofly drive` (optionally with `left` / `right` / `up`): it drives and you hammer. Stand next to it: it jumps, you hammer it up, and it hooks you. Change the direction mid-flight with `Claude left` / `Claude right` / `Claude up`.
- `Claude hammerhit` (default `right`, also `left`): for freeze floors, e.g. Stronghold's opening. Whenever you are frozen (or about to be) and it is free, it hooks you out and hammers you once you are clear. Then it's your turn: it usually lands in the freeze, so hook it out and hammer it. At a freeze wall it aleds you through, and you pull it through after. On the ground it walks in the goal direction. `Claude left` / `Claude right` switch the direction. To play Stronghold: `HOOKBOT_MAP=Stronghold scripts/run_hookbot_server.sh` (the map goes in `build-sim/maps/`).
  - Frozen and lying still, it holds the direction that makes a hammer take it furthest towards the goal (`FrozenHoldDir`): the hammer unfreezes it for one tick, and that tick its input counts before the freeze has it again. It simulates your hammer from where you are (or from every free spot in reach), with left, nothing and right, and says "hammer me, I'm holding right/left" when one is clearly better. Stronghold: on top of the 3-tile post right of the ledge, holding right is the difference between falling past the post's edge and landing back on it.
  - You frozen for good (lying in freeze) and no rescue works from where it stands (a second of planning at each new spot): it goes on by itself along its line, to where one does (`Alone` in `Play`). Its jump search (`SoloSearch`) then also takes paths through freeze onto a spot outside it where it thaws (the frozen ticks count against it), and besides braking at its aim point, it tries aiming short and coasting, so the speed carries it on through the freeze. Stronghold, you at the bottom of the shaft, it on the platform below the ledge: it jumps right, passes the freeze band just beside the block in it and drifts onto the pillar in the freeze floor below, thaws, jumps left to the ledge under the shaft's side opening and hooks you out (about 9 s in `SimMapBots.Alone`; rank 1: 6.5 s). While you're frozen for good, your position doesn't move the route on (you may lie right next to later waypoints), and the "nearer to a later stretch" rule only counts stretches it can reach through open air.
  - Solo parts with the hook (`SwingSearch`): when no plain jump gets it anywhere, it plans moves with the hook: from the ground or the air, it tries short steps (no hook steering left/right/neither, with or without a jump; a new hook on a hookable tile in reach while steering, held 6-32 ticks; or keep the hook it's on while steering another way, 4-14 ticks), keeps the best 8 after each (a beam search, up to 8 steps deep in 200 ms), never through freeze or within 4 px of it. A step's end is scored by where the speed carries it if it lets go of the hook (still steering the same way, which keeps the speed in the air; with the air jump too if it still has one), not by where it is. A plan that ends safe (letting go, it lands and brakes to a stop without coming near freeze) wins over one that gets further; if none does, it follows the first 30 ticks of the best one and plans on from there, while flying (the next search starts before the plan runs out; between plans it keeps steering the way it was). These searches aim 7 waypoints ahead (`m_SoloLead`, a field of its own: with the usual 3, hovering at a goal nearby scored as well as flying through it fast). Stronghold after the shaft: it swings along the hookable blocks in the ceiling over the 30-tile freeze band, back left through the middle corridor at 22 px/tick, through the gap in the lower freeze band on the hookable block in it (grabbing while still moving left, switching to right mid-swing, out at about 20 px/tick) and along the bottom corridor onto the floor at x 413-427 (about 5 s; rank 1 about 5 s too).
  - The jump search's spots: only ones open air connects it to (a clean jump can't reach anything else; the spots beyond a 12-tile freeze column used up its time), and no spot with a foot inside a block (against its side, not on it). A run-up stops a tee short of the partner standing behind it (bumping into it pushed both off the plan). Stronghold near the start, you on the ledge at x 168-178: a run-up, a jump and an air jump over a 24-tile gap onto the very edge of a block with a freeze column standing on it (x 202.56-202.97: one foot on the block, the body outside the freeze).
  - Through freeze on its own: a swing plan step that enters freeze isn't rejected if the frozen fall then lands outside the freeze and it thaws there; that ends the plan, safe, the frozen ticks counting against it. From the ground, a swing plan that doesn't end safe only comes after the jump search through freeze (`SoloSearch` with `AllowFrozen`: one spot per place there, ground under its middle). Stronghold after the ceiling swings, standing on the block right of a 10-wide shaft: it drifts left while falling and passes the 1-tile freeze column on the shaft's left side at the opening behind it (rows 220-225, air-jumping there), lands frozen on the block in the pocket at (237, 225.5) and thaws. Before, both searches refused any freeze, a swing plan hovering on its air jump won, and the old drop rule walked it off the edge into the freeze at the bottom of the shaft.
  - You free but far behind (10+ tiles further from the goal than it): it doesn't run off. A solo move in progress ends where it can land safely, it starts no new one from the ground and doesn't drop into freeze, until you catch up.
  - Before any rescue plan, a direct rule (`Hammerhit`): standing on the ground outside the freeze with the partner frozen, if it's in hammer reach and a hammer from here makes it come to rest outside the freeze (8 px clear on both sides), hammer now; if it's still falling and will come to rest in reach, stand still and wait for it (dropping any plan). Rescue plans that deliver the partner within 8 px of freeze score 150 less (thrown to the far end of a 3-wide free part for the progress, it came to rest a pixel from the next column and got hooked back).
  - Every rescue search first tries standing where it is, no hook (jumping at some tick, maybe), hammering whenever the partner is in reach (checked every tick); a rescue where it never leaves the ground scores 100 more. A frozen partner flying through the air counts as thawing by itself if its fall, doing nothing, comes to rest outside the freeze (not while a rescue plan is running). A rescue plan dropped with it in the air gets a way down that keeps it out of the freeze (`SafeFallDir`, if any). While the partner isn't frozen yet (only about to be), it only carries out rescue plans that keep it on the ground (not when the partner is cut off and to be pulled through: it's free there by design, and the rule kept it from ever helping you up through a freeze band). Following a route, the walking rule's drop into freeze only goes where the route goes near (it walked off a ledge onto a freeze floor 16 tiles down where the route jumps a gap high above). Still flaky: Stronghold's 3-wide edge by a freeze column at x 202.56-202.97 (y 27.5), the partner frozen at the column's foot. Lying at 204.6 it's in reach (61 px) and the direct rule hammers it to 206.3; a few px further in (204.9) it's out of reach, and the plans that leave the edge often end with it in the freeze. From a settled state (the partner lying at 204.6) it picks the right rescue, one hammer from standing, which puts the partner at 206.3 outside the freeze (`FromState`: `FS_FIRST=1 FS_BOT=6486,881,0,0 FS_PARTNER=6547,881`); with the partner still falling in, re-plans switch it to plans that leave the edge, and it often falls.
  - Rescues by the hook alone: when no hammer rescue has come up for half a second (`m_NoRescueSince`), the rescue planner also takes plans without a hammer: it lets go of the hook once the hook has dragged you out of the freeze, and you come to rest outside it and thaw (scored like a delivery, minus the freeze time you have left). Its plans also aim the hook half a tee above you (the hook starts 42 px out, so that clears a corner in the way). Stronghold, you frozen on the freeze strip above the tunnel, it in the tunnel below: it runs left, jumps, hooks you at the top of the jump and keeps running, which drags you over the edge and down the freeze column into the tunnel. Before, every rescue plan had to end with a hammer, so it found none there and went on alone along the route, away from you. Taking them right away made the opening slower (another rescue comes up a moment later there).
  - In that stage it also tries rescues from a jump (`PlanRescue`'s hook families, first, with their own cursor from job to job, 100 ms a job, the plan anchored 7 ticks ahead): walk to a spot within 10 tiles, jump, maybe air-jump 8-24 ticks later, hook 4-36 ticks after the jump (aimed at you, or 24/40 px above), then hold a direction to drag you out; the regular families can't (hook times from the plan's start up to tick 25, one direction all the way, one jump). Stronghold, you frozen in a freeze notch in the side of a pillar 18 tiles above the floor: jump, air jump, hook at the top aiming above you, which drags you out of the notch. Once such a plan's hook is about to fire it isn't improved any more (the improvements changed when the hook fired, and the hook in flight missed); a hook-only plan is carried through to its end even when you count as thawing by yourself meanwhile; one that leaves it frozen itself doesn't count, and its own fall after letting go is followed until it lands (not 60 ticks: a 55-tile fall into freeze went unseen). Rescue plans are played with their own aim (the executor used to keep aiming at your middle, so the aim above you never happened live).
- `Claude goal <x> <y>` (tiles) or `Claude goal here`: **go there together, with whatever it takes** (`MODE_PLAY`, also `Claude play`). Every tick it picks:
  - **Rescue:** you're frozen (or will be) and it's free → the hammerhit rescue planner, aleds included.
  - **Fly:** you're both free in the air, the goal is reachable from there through open air (a second distance map where freeze is impassable, so a freeze wall in the way means aled, not fly), and climbing gets you closer → pseudofly. The upper tee drives, the lower one hammers. As the driver, the bot steers along the distance map, so it finds openings (Stronghold's ceiling gap). As the hammerer, it only kicks while climbing still helps, so the pair hovers at the goal. So if it's below you, drive; if it's above you, hammer it.
  - **Otherwise:** walk or fall towards the goal.
  - If the fly fails (someone freezes), it goes back to rescuing.
- `Claude route`: follow the route for the current map (`hookbot/routes/<map>.txt` in the data or save directory; a downloaded map's `_<sha256>` file name suffix is ignored, one `x y` waypoint in tiles per line) in play mode, moving the goal on by itself. Routes come from rank 1 runs: `scripts/demo_route.py <dump.tsv> <out.txt> --from <run start s>` samples one tee's path every 12 tiles (`data/hookbot/routes/Stronghold.txt`: 273 waypoints). `scripts/fetch_routes.py` does it for every map on ddnet.org/watch except the Dummy, Race and Fun categories (Brutal first; it falls back to the fastest watchable run when rank 1 is missing; it prefers a rank 1 team run of two tees, cuts the path from the last start tile touch to the first finish tile touch, and skips maps that already have a route). CMake globs `data/hookbot/routes/*.txt`, so new files need no `EXPECTED_DATA` entry. A route is only the path the players took: parts that need teleporters, weapons other than the hammer, or techniques the bot doesn't have will still stop it.
  - A route file can hold one path per tee of the run, each after a `# path` line (`scripts/demo_route.py --cid 0,1`; Stronghold's has both: 254 and 252 waypoints). The bot follows one of them and switches to another when it's clearly on that one: its nearest visible stretch (line of sight, not through walls) is within 5 tiles and 4+ tiles closer than its own (lines running side by side made it flip back and forth). `Claude route` starts on the line it can see closest to it, checked every 10 ticks, at most once a second. It says "I'm on the other line". Where a team splits up, it thus plays the other tee's part (Stronghold after the fly: frozen on top of the 3-tile post right of the ledge, the shaft line is behind the post, the other tee's line runs down the freeze column right next to it, towards the open area below).
  - It starts at the earliest waypoint within 16 tiles of you (or not much farther than the nearest one). Stronghold's finish is right next to its spawn, so "the nearest waypoint" made it start at the last one and walk to the finish (`claude_wont_start`). It moves on once one of you is within 6 tiles of the current waypoint and both within 14, or once you're nearer to a later stretch of the route than to the current one (a slower team takes a different line: rank 1 flies where you hammerhit along the floor). It says each new waypoint in chat.
  - It picks its place on the route again (like at the start, and says "back to waypoint ...") after a kill, respawn or teleport (a tee jumping more than 4 tiles in one tick), or when you're both more than 30 tiles from the stretch of route it was following (`pretty_big_regression`: after a kill at Stronghold it kept the waypoint of the previous attempt, and from the bottom of the first shaft that goal led back towards the start).
  - It also moves on by progress: once one of you is closer to the current goal (by the walking distances) than a waypoint before it is, that waypoint counts as passed. In `claude_keep_going_towards_startline` you dropped straight down the first shaft where rank 1 flew diagonally, never came within 14 tiles of those waypoints, and the goal stayed behind you, so every rescue dragged you back toward the start.
  - The goal maps aim 3 waypoints ahead (`m_RouteLead`). A fast run's waypoints often hang in mid-air, and a goal right there made rescues fling the partner up at it. The open-air map (used for flying) aims at the farthest of those that's reachable through air from the current waypoint, since the run may pass freeze further on (Stronghold's freeze ceiling after the fly).
  - A waypoint in freeze (the run passed it frozen, like Stronghold's freeze ceiling after the fly): the open-air map aims at the nearest open tile next to it that's reachable through air from where you both are. So after falling back down, the bot flies back up to just below the ceiling (`claude_fell_down_and_refused_to_go_back_up`: before, the flying goal was unreachable, so it never flew again).
  - No rescue plan yet (e.g. you're frozen 50 tiles below): while it plans, it heads for you, taking whichever of left, right or standing still gets it closest in half a second (never into freeze or a kill tile). So it drops down a gap after you and plans the rescue on the way down. Before, it stood still, since no rescue fits the 80-tick lookahead from that high (`stuck_after_first_fly`).
  - Thrown at freeze the route goes through (rising faster than 8 px/tick with freeze within 4 tiles above): it steers the way the route continues beyond it (Stronghold: up through the freeze ceiling and right onto the blocks). Before, it kept steering to stay under you, and killed the sideways speed of your throw.
  - You're free but cut off (not reachable from the bot through open air within 24 tiles, and at least 200 px further from the goal): it treats you as needing a rescue. It stays above you, and when you jump into hook range it pulls you up through the freeze and hammers you free (`claude_kills_itself_instead_of_helping_me`: on the blocks above Stronghold's freeze ceiling it used to walk on and fall into the freeze strip, leaving you below).
  - After the hammer, it only steers its own fall until it lands, then the walking check takes over (`claude_walks_left_for_no_reason_after_saving_me`: it held left for the full 1.2 s after landing on the blocks, walked off their end into the freeze patch, and fell to the bottom).
  - Back down below a freeze ceiling the route goes through, with the flying goal set while you were up above it: when you're both free in the air and it can't reach the flying goal through air, it picks that goal again from where you are (at most once a second). If nothing ahead on the route can be reached through air, it flies to the spot you can reach through air that's closest to the goal (Stronghold: just under the freeze ceiling, where you can throw it). This only applies when the route goes up from that spot. For a freeze wall to the side it would just hover at the wall, which is how it got stuck at Stronghold's first aled; an aled needs one of you frozen. Before, it only hammerhit at the bottom.
  - Its own fall after the hammer: if some steering keeps it out of the freeze (it lands on something), it takes that; plans where it falls into freeze cost 150 points. And freeing you onto solid ground outside the freeze gets a 100 point bonus.
  - A frozen partner lying still outside the freeze thaws by itself, so the bot waits instead of rescuing.
  - Deliveries: a rescue plan may also count if the partner isn't freed but comes to rest (frozen) outside the freeze, where it thaws; the bot may hammer a partner that's lying in freeze. Scores rank freed-and-landed-on-solid-ground (+250) above delivered (+150) above freed in the air (air time and height, up to +188), before progress (`weird2`: you landed just inside a freeze column next to the bot on the edge; it dragged you both off the edge, instead of hammering you through the column to the safe spot behind it).
  - Gestures: a hammer swing of yours aimed up that can't hit the bot (more than 70 px away) means "let's fly": for 5 s it flies wherever you both can. If you're both standing, it jumps first (it drives, you hammer), even where climbing wouldn't obviously help. It answers with a nod (its aim up and down, when it isn't aiming a hook or hammer).
  - Moving on by itself (`SoloMove`): when you're both standing safely and nobody needs rescuing, it looks for a spot further along where it can stand without freezing, within 30 tiles sideways and 12 up or down. That includes the very edges of platforms: a tee can stand with its center just off a block, and the center is what freezes. Stronghold's example is the left edge of the block after the freeze ceiling, x 202.56-202.97, right next to a freeze column.
    - For each spot it simulates variants: back up 0, 4 or 8 tiles for a run-up; jump at once or at the end of the platform; air-jump 6-33 ticks later or not. It steers to the spot's x, and takes the one that lands furthest along without touching freeze or kill tiles.
    - The search runs on a worker thread (`SoloSearch`, up to 100 ms) while it stands still; then it plays back the simulated inputs, and drops the plan if it's more than 3 px off. On the client's main thread (4 ms slices) it made the game stutter before each jump.
    - It jumps a few ticks before the end of the platform, not on the last tick, and a plan counts only if its first jump is from the ground (`claude_loses_dj_at_jump_then_we_get_stuck`: live it jumped one tick late, walked off, and the jump became the air jump).
    - With nowhere to jump to, it walks as before (e.g. off the start ledge into the shaft).
    - A move has to land at least 2 tiles closer to the goal than where it stands (`weird`: a "move" 2 tiles over to the wall won each time, so it jumped in place over and over before your throw).
    - The spots are tried best first, and the search stops once a jump works.
    - `claude_doesnt_know_what_to_do_next`: it stood at the end of the blocks while you jumped on to the edge. Now it lands on that edge from 2 of 3 starting spots in the sim.
  - The cut-off check (`AirConnected`) only looks 24 tiles around the bot. Beyond that it counts as "can't tell", not "cut off": otherwise it turned back to rescue you in the middle of its jump.
  - Walking on the ground: it tries half a second of walking left or right in its lookahead, letting go of the direction once off the ground as it does live, then 2 s of coasting. In this order: a move that gets closer to the goal without freezing; else a drop into freeze; else stay.
    - A drop is allowed where it lands closer to the goal (measured just above the freeze: Stronghold's first shaft). It's also allowed where it lands at most 400 px further when you're not behind it: you're both stuck at the same ledge (Simple Down's spawn) and someone has to go first. Never where it throws progress away (Stronghold's ceiling gap, ~1600 px), and never off the edge while you're still coming (it waits).
    - It has to be a real fall, at least 2 tiles down, not walking into a freeze wall. and takes whichever ends closest to the goal; freeze or kill tiles rule a direction out. The old rule, move to whichever side is closer to the goal, walked it off the upper room's floor into the ceiling gap below it (a 50-tile fall).
  - New goal maps are built on a thread, and a plan in progress survives the switch (switching mid-aled used to drop the pull-through).
- `Claude hammerhit` with a goal set: hammerhit towards the goal instead of just left or right (no flying). The bot builds a map of walking distances to the goal (`CHookBotGoalField`: through air, through freeze at 8x the cost, never through walls or kill tiles), so tunnels that turn and drops work. For Simple Down's spawn segment: `HOOKBOT_MAP="Simple Down" scripts/run_hookbot_server.sh`, then `Claude goal 188 86` (the room after the third tunnel) and `Claude hammerhit`.
- `Claude come` (server-side bot only): teleports it next to you (it also teleports when you start a mode from more than about 19 tiles away).
- `Claude stop`

## Hot reload
With `cl_hookbot_hotreload 1`, the client loads the bot's brain (`src/game/hookbot.cpp`/`.h`) from `libhookbot-hot.so` next to the executable and loads it again whenever the file changes. So you can change the bot while playing: `cd build-client && ninja -j2 hookbot-hot` (just that file, a few seconds). About half a second after the build, the dummy echoes "reloaded my brain (#n)".
- The new brain starts from scratch, and the client replays the chat commands since the last `stop` on it, quietly (`Claude route` picks its waypoint again from where you both are).
- The client only talks to the brain through `IHookBot` (`src/game/hookbot_plugin.h`), so the brain's members and layout can change freely. Changing `hookbot_plugin.h`, the `MODE_*` numbers, `gamecore.h`, `collision.h` or anything else the client itself uses needs a client rebuild. A size check refuses a plugin that doesn't match the running client.
- Old plugins stay loaded (their brain's threads may still be finishing). The loaded copies (`libhookbot-hot.so.<n>.loaded`) are deleted right after loading.
- macOS only for now: the plugin takes the rest of the game from the executable (`-undefined dynamic_lookup`). Linux would also need the client linked with `-rdynamic`, Windows a different approach.

## How it works
- **Hookfly** (`CHookBotBrain::Hookfly`): the upper tee hooks the lower one once its own rise has slowed (vy > -12; from the ground: at its apex). It lets go when the partner has passed it, and moves away from the partner while hooking. As a rider it steps aside so it passes beside you. These are the `SimFly` rules; `SimBot.TuneHookfly` grid-searched the bot's parameters.
- **Lookahead** (`CHookBotSim`): both tees stepped with the shared `CCharacterCore` plus freeze, in the server's tick order. One tick matches the server exactly (checked in `SimBot.Aled` with `SIM_DBG=1`). About 1.2 µs per step. Your future inputs come from a model of a hookflyer (`PartnerModel`): hook the bot up once it is below you and your rise slows, step away while hooking, let go once it has passed.
- **Aled:**
  1. Energy management (`HoldIsRisky`): don't hook one more tick if that would put either tee into the freeze within the next half-cycle (a clean pass through is allowed).
  2. Launch (`TryLaunch`): every tick while hooking you (or with you in hammer range), it tries four inputs: let go or hold on, with or without a hammer. It launches only if you would come out above the freeze and a full dive plan exists.
  3. Dive (`PlanDive`): searches hook start, jump tick and direction. The hammer fires by itself on the first tick the bot's last move touched freeze, and only counts if you are beyond the freeze, in reach, and not refrozen for 12 ticks after. It replans when reality leaves the planned path by more than 2 px.
  - Planning is capped at 30 ms per server tick (`m_PlanBudgetUs`); it averages about 0.3 ms.

- **Hammerhit** (`Hammerhit`, `PlanRescue`): a search, since the map decides everything.
  - Candidate plans: a direction held throughout, an optional jump tick, a hook start, and hook until the hammer or let go early (a fling). The hammer fires by itself on the best tick where you are in reach, out of the freeze, and your last move didn't touch it.

  - Score: your progress in the goal direction 30 ticks after the hammer, minus 1 per tick of waiting, plus 400 for an aled (you come out beyond a freeze line that the bot stays behind), plus your clearance above the freeze at the hammer (up to 128 px), minus 80 if the bot's own last move touched freeze. ## Compute
Measured on the whole of Stronghold with `HH_DET=2000` (the run that finishes; `runs/fulld.sh`), one core, both brains in one process, 429 s of game:

| | Brain time | Per simulated tick |
|---|---|---|
| Before (2026-10-04 morning) | 1068 s (2.5x slower than the game) | 3.9 us |
| Now, each brain searching for itself (`HH_NOSHARE=1`) | 215 s | 0.79 us |
| Now | **114 s** (0.27x the game's time) | 0.80 us |

Every change below leaves the game exactly as it was: the same plans, the same route messages, tick for tick (checked by diffing the full run's log).

Where it went:
- **Team searches: 90%.** About 70% of their ticks were searches that found nothing and ran out their whole budget, e.g. fall-catch tries every 10 ticks in the opening pit (26 in a row, then one found).
- **Goal fields: 7%,** 5240 full-map searches for 254 waypoints.
- **Reachability checks:** CanReach and CanRescue.
- **The rest:** rescues, solo swings and finish searches, under 5% together.

What changed:
- **The team searches are shared between the two brains** (`TEAM_CALL`, `TeamCall`). Both plan the same team move from the same state at the same tick, and every planner is a static function of the state, the goal fields and the budget. So the first brain to get there searches and the other takes the result, keyed by the state dump, the call site, the fields and the route. `HH_NOSHARE=1` makes each search for itself. This only works with both bots in one process (a server).
- **Player slots:** `CWorldCore::m_aActive` / `m_NumActive` optionally list the slots in use, and `CCharacterCore`'s loops over the players use the list when it's kept. The bot's sim keeps it (2 of 128 slots); the game doesn't, and loops over all 128 as before. Also, `Move` picks the players it can collide with once per move, not once per pixel of it. Those loops were 55% of all instructions.
- **Sim freeze check:** `CHookBotSim::Step` reuses the previous step's freeze check of the same move. `PathTouchesFreeze` returns at once when no freeze tile is in the move's box.
- **`CCollision::MoveBox`** skips its box tests when nothing solid is anywhere the box gets to on the move. Otherwise it reuses the last test while the box's four corners stay in the same tiles. `IntersectLine` checks each tile once, not each pixel. `GetMoveRestrictions` returns at once on a map with no stoppers and no doors. `HasDeathTiles` lets the sim skip its kill-tile check on maps without any.
- **Build flag:** `-fno-semantic-interposition` (`CMakeLists.txt`). With `-fPIC`, GCC inlined none of the collision lookups.
- **Goal fields are cached** by what they're built from: tile, freeze cost and line. That covers both brains, and every re-pick. They're built with a bucket queue: every step costs 32 or more, so 32-wide buckets give the same distances as a heap, about 2x faster. "Can open air get from here to there" is answered from the map's open-air regions, labelled once (`CHookBotAirReach`), not from a full field per question. `HookBotMapCheck` (called by `CHookBotBrain::Init`) drops these caches when the map's tiles change.
- **The reachability checks** read a per-map table of tile kinds (`TileInfo`), not 81x81 tiles of the map per call.
- **The joint search's nodes** hold their own step and a link to their parent, not their whole history: up to 576 children per node copied it. They're sorted by index, not moved about.

Still the biggest costs: the joint search (41 s), fall-catch (29 s, 17 s of it in the tries from a frozen fall), climb, leap, and route upkeep (10 s).
